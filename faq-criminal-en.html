51I have been summoned by an investigation authority. What should I do before the appointment?
Direct answer

Identify the type of order, whether a summons or arrest warrant, the provision alleged against you and the complaint number. Do not attend alone without a lawyer when giving statements before the investigating officer and Investigation Court judge, to avoid unexpected pretrial detention.

Legal reference

Articles 87, 92 and 123 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971.

Required documents

The official notice, appointment of a lawyer, and advance preparation of exculpatory arguments and documents refuting the allegation.

52A family member is detained. What information does a lawyer need to begin following the case?
Direct answer

The police station or security authority holding the person, the competent court and Investigation Court judge, the provision under which the person is detained and the detention date are needed to attend the investigation and immediately seek release on secured bail.

Legal reference

Articles 109–119 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971.

Required documents

The detainee's full four-part name and mother's name, the case number and a bail application supported by financial or property security.

53I heard that an arrest warrant or other action exists against me. How can I verify this officially?
Direct answer

Appoint a lawyer under a power of attorney for criminal matters to enquire with the competent Investigation Court, the criminal computer records section and warrant-enforcement units. This establishes officially whether a summons or arrest warrant exists and the complaint details, without risking direct attendance.

Legal reference

Articles 99 onwards of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971.

Required documents

A general power of attorney for legal representation or one specifically covering criminal complaints, and the client's full personal particulars.

54A relative is detained. What determines whether release on bail is possible?
Direct answer

The offence and its penalty determine the position. For a misdemeanour or infraction punishable by imprisonment of three years or less, bail is mandatory by law except in exceptional cases. For felonies it is subject to the Investigation Court judge's discretion, while offences punishable by death are categorically non-bailable.

Legal reference

Articles 109 and 110 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971.

Required documents

A bail application addressed to the Investigation Court judge, a civil-service guarantor with valid identification and salary confirmation, or property security.

55I was assaulted and want to complain. Which documents and evidence are needed?
Direct answer

Prepare a complaint to the competent Investigation Court judge, obtain an immediate forensic medical examination recording the injuries and incapacity period, identify witnesses and retrieve nearby CCTV recordings to establish the criminal incident.

Legal reference

Articles 413–415 of the Penal Code and Articles 1–9 of the Criminal Procedure Law.

Required documents

The complaint, preliminary forensic medical report, CCTV recordings and details of witnesses who were present.

56Someone filed a complaint against me, and I deny it. What should I prepare for my lawyer?
Direct answer

Prepare an alibi through CCTV, attendance records or witnesses establishing that you were elsewhere when the act occurred. Request closure of the investigation and referral of the complainant for trial for making a false report under Article 243 of the Penal Code.

Legal reference

Article 243 of the amended Penal Code No. 111 of 1969.

Required documents

Evidence of presence elsewhere, including official records, tickets or CCTV, witnesses and documents refuting the allegation.

57I am accused of forging a signature or document. Which materials should defence counsel review first?
Direct answer

Examine the original disputed document, request formally obtained handwriting samples and technical comparison by the Handwriting and Documents Examination Section of the Criminal Evidence Directorate, and challenge photocopies that are unsuitable for legally valid comparison.

Legal reference

Iraqi Evidence Law No. 107 of 1979 and Articles 286–298 of the Penal Code.

Required documents

The original disputed document, original signature and handwriting specimens predating the dispute, and officially obtained handwriting samples from the accused.

58I have a case involving a cheque. What determines its legal route?
Direct answer

The criminal route is a complaint for issuing a cheque without sufficient funds under Article 459 of the Penal Code, supported by a bank rejection notice confirming insufficient funds or withdrawal of the balance. The civil or enforcement route is to lodge the cheque directly with the Enforcement Directorate to recover its value as a preferential debt.

Legal reference

Article 459 of Penal Code No. 111 of 1969 and Commerce Law No. 30 of 1984.

Required documents

The original cheque, the drawee bank's rejection letter and the cheque holder's identity document.

59I was robbed and know the person or have recordings. How should I organise a complaint?
Direct answer

Submit a complaint to the Investigation Court judge, provide the CCTV clips on a documented flash drive for examination by criminal-evidence specialists, and record witness statements and the site inspection, traces and fingerprints so an immediate arrest and search warrant can be sought.

Legal reference

Articles 440–446 of the amended Penal Code No. 111 of 1969.

Required documents

A list of stolen property and estimated values, video recordings, witnesses and the police report.

60Someone threatens to publish my photos or conversations. Which legal steps require urgent assessment?
Direct answer

Do not pay the blackmailer or delete messages. Immediately complain to the Investigation Court and the cybercrime office to seek a judicial order tracing the blackmailer's telephone number and IP address and apprehending the person in the act.

Legal reference

Articles 430–432 of the Penal Code and the Iraqi telecommunications regulation law.

Required documents

Complete, preserved screenshots, the blackmailer's numbers and accounts, and the explicit threatening messages.

61My account was stolen, or someone impersonates me. Which evidence is needed to assess a complaint?
Direct answer

Document the impersonating account links, preserve screenshots of posts and messages, identify the email address or phone number associated with the original account, and make a formal complaint to the Investigation Court judge so the National Security Service and telecommunications companies can be contacted.

Legal reference

Iraqi Penal Code No. 111 of 1969, including forgery, fraud and threat offences.

Required documents

Page links, images of posts and messages, and official identification establishing ownership of the original account.

62Someone published offensive statements about me on social media. What determines whether legal action is possible?
Direct answer

The publication must contain express defamatory allegations affecting honour or social standing under Article 433 of the Penal Code, or insults under Article 434. A complaint is brought before the Investigation Court, with a civil claim before the Court of First Instance for non-material harm.

Legal reference

Articles 433 and 434 of the amended Penal Code No. 111 of 1969.

Required documents

Record the posts or recordings, their links and dates before deletion, identify the publisher and submit the complaint.

63I have messages or audio recordings. What determines whether they can be used as evidence?
Direct answer

Text messages and electronic application conversations constitute legally recognised written evidence under the Electronic Signature and Electronic Transactions Law. Audio recordings require prior judicial permission and referral to a criminal-evidence expert for voiceprint comparison, and are treated as corroborating judicial evidence.

Legal reference

Electronic Signature and Electronic Transactions Law No. 78 of 2012 and the Evidence Law.

Required documents

The original phone containing the recordings and messages for direct forensic technical examination.

64The complainant wants to withdraw. What determines the effect on the case?
Direct answer

For offences dependent on a personal complaint under Article 3 of the Criminal Procedure Law, such as insult and minor assault, withdrawal stops proceedings and extinguishes the case. For public-prosecution offences such as theft and felonies, the public claim remains and withdrawal is a mitigating factor.

Legal reference

Articles 3 and 300 of the Criminal Procedure Law and Articles 130–132 of the Penal Code.

Required documents

A withdrawal instrument authenticated by a notary public or an express statement of withdrawal and settlement before the Investigation Court judge or trial court.

65A juvenile is involved in a case. What information should the family prepare before consulting a lawyer?
Direct answer

Establish the juvenile's exact date of birth to determine coverage under the Juvenile Welfare Law, between ages 9 and 18. Trial must be before the Juvenile Court in the presence of the guardian and social researcher; hearings are private and measures are rehabilitative.

Legal reference

Juvenile Welfare Law No. 76 of 1983, as amended.

Required documents

The juvenile's birth certificate or national ID card, investigation papers, and social and psychological assessment reports.

146I was summoned as a witness and fear the case could turn against me. What should the lawyer know?
Direct answer

A witness may be accompanied by a lawyer. Review the charge and provision alleged against the accused carefully. Under Article 88 of the Criminal Procedure Law, a witness may decline testimony that would personally incriminate them or disclose a professional secret they are obliged to protect.

Legal reference

Articles 88, 94 and 123 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971.

Required documents

The witness summons, complaint number and a statement of the personal or employment relationship with the parties.

147A relative faces a drugs case. Which seizure records and test reports are needed?
Direct answer

Distinguish strictly between personal use under Article 32, a misdemeanour allowing bail or admission to a treatment facility, and trafficking or distribution under Articles 27 and 28, serious felonies. Check the legality of search and arrest authority and reconcile the substance's type and weight with criminal-evidence records and blood and urine examination reports.

Legal reference

Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Law No. 50 of 2017 and the Criminal Procedure Law.

Required documents

The seizure and search report, Forensic Medicine Institute substance and biological-marker analysis, and preliminary investigation papers.

148My name resembles a wanted person's name. What is needed to correct the confusion officially?
Direct answer

Obtain a 1957 family civil-register extract establishing the full five-part name, mother's name, surname, register and page numbers and address. Apply to the Investigation Court judge for an official clarification letter to checkpoints, border crossings and the Central Computer Section.

Legal reference

Supreme Judicial Council and Ministry of Interior instructions on similar names, and the Civil Status Law.

Required documents

The national ID card, 1957 civil-register extract, residence card and a clarification application to the competent judge.

149My complaint was closed or rejected. What determines whether I can challenge the decision?
Direct answer

Challenge by cassation before the Criminal Court acting in its cassation capacity within 30 days of service or explanation of the decision. The lawyer submits a reasoned petition identifying investigation shortcomings and seeks quashing and remittal to complete evidence and hear witnesses.

Legal reference

Articles 249–265 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971.

Required documents

The closure or refusal decision, original complaint and reasoned cassation petition.

150A relative is serving a sentence. What must be reviewed to assess amnesty eligibility?
Direct answer

Check that the conviction provision is not among excluded offences under the amnesty laws. Calculate the sentence and time served, including three-quarters of the term with good conduct for conditional release, and confirm payment of fines and restitution and authenticated withdrawal by the complainant.

Legal reference

Criminal Procedure Law, Articles 331 onwards on conditional release, and the applicable general and special amnesty laws.

Required documents

The final judgment, prison good-conduct certificate and officially authenticated complainant withdrawal.
