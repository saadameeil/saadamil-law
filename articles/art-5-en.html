Challenging Bank Account Freezes, Bank Guarantee Calls, Government Contract Terminations and Public Indemnification Orders in Iraq

✍️ By: Lawyer Saad Amil (ID 188572) 📅 Published: 2026-11-26 📍 Baghdad, Republic of Iraq
How corporations, contractors and foreign investors protect bank balances, performance guarantees and investment licences before Iraq's Economic Crime Courts, Commercial Courts and Administrative Court.

Corporations, private banks, licensed exchange companies and contractors operating in Iraq face acute operational exposure when bank accounts are frozen or multi-billion-dinar letters of guarantee are called. Under Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Law No. 39 of 2015 and Central Bank of Iraq compliance directives, lifting account holds before the Economic Crime Investigation Court requires a rigorous evidentiary audit trail linking every remittance to underlying supply contracts, customs declarations and tax filings.

In public infrastructure and supply contracts governed by Government Contracts Implementation Instructions No. 2 of 2014, contractors frequently suffer losses by continuing site work informally without serving formal notices recording handover obstructions, delayed engineering approvals or unpaid progress certificates. When a ministry moves to withdraw work and call performance bonds, swift action before the Commercial Court or Court of First Instance is critical to stay guarantee liquidation, prove administrative fault and prevent blacklisting.

Where state contracts or public funds trigger scrutiny, defence strategy must strictly distinguish criminal investigations before Integrity Courts under amended Integrity Commission Law No. 30 of 2011 and Penal Code Articles 340–341 from administrative liability orders issued under Indemnification Law No. 31 of 2015 and Instructions No. 2 of 2017. Administrative indemnification orders are subject to mandatory administrative grievance followed by annulment litigation before the State Council's Administrative Court within strict statutory time limits.

Finally, capital protection in Iraq requires proactive statutory structuring across tax, investment and commercial property: filing reasoned tax objections within 21 days under amended Income Tax Law No. 113 of 1982, safeguarding investment licences under Law No. 13 of 2006, registering commercial Musataha agreements (up to 50 years) in the Property Registration Directorate under Civil Code Article 1266, and deploying urgent precautionary attachment under Civil Procedure Law Article 231 to secure high-value commercial debts.
