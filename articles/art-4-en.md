# Judicial Pathways for Challenging Death and Life Sentences and Applying Iraq's Second General Amnesty Amendment Law No. 2 of 2025

**Author:** Attorney Saad Firas Amil (Iraqi Bar Association ID: 188572)  
**Published:** 2026-11-26  
**Canonical URL:** https://saadamil.com/articles/art-4-en.html

> A statutory guide to advanced felony defence before Criminal Courts and the Federal Court of Cassation, applying the Second General Amnesty Amendment Law No. 2 of 2025, and reducing final sentences under the Iraqi Penal Code and Criminal Procedure Law.

Challenging capital and life-imprisonment convictions in Iraq requires a disciplined audit extending from initial police seizure records and committal orders to the trial court's evidentiary reasoning. In homicide prosecutions under Penal Code No. 111 of 1969, the line separating capital murder under Article 406(1) from intentional homicide under Article 405 or fatal assault under Article 410 turns on disproving calm premeditation and reconciling forensic autopsy findings, weapon type and wound location with actual criminal intent.

Following enactment of the Second Amendment to the General Amnesty Law, Law No. 2 of 2025 (published in Iraqi Official Gazette issue 4814 on 18 February 2025), specific statutory remedies opened for prisoners and detainees. Foremost is the right to petition appellate judicial committees for re-investigation or retrial where a conviction relied on confessions extracted under coercion in breach of Article 37(1)(c) of the Constitution and Article 127 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971, or rested solely on secret informants or uncorroborated co-defendant statements contrary to Article 125 CPC.

In prosecutions under Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Law No. 50 of 2017, Law No. 2 of 2025 continues to exclude drug trafficking (Articles 27 and 28) while extending amnesty coverage to personal-use possession where net forensic laboratory weight does not exceed 50 grams, absent disqualifying prior convictions or prior amnesty use. Separately, Penal Code Article 101 protects bona-fide third-party owners whose vehicles, properties or funds were seized without their knowledge of the offence.

Even after judgments become final, Iraqi legislation provides four distinct post-conviction mechanisms: sentence merger under Penal Code Article 142 (executing only the severest penalty where inseparable offences share one purpose); enforcing the 25-year statutory maximum on consecutive prison terms and absorption of lesser detention sentences under Article 143(A) and (C); retrial petitions under Articles 270–279 CPC upon discovery of decisive new exculpatory evidence or proof of perjured testimony; and conditional release under Article 331 CPC after completing the statutory fraction of the term with good conduct.
