266Someone insults me or accuses me and my business on Facebook or TikTok. What is needed to assess the posts and harm?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Legal classification: Public defamation and insult under Articles 433 and 434, read with Article 19(3) of the Penal Code concerning publicity through social media. For the lawyer: Direct post and profile links, accurate screenshots of posts and comments with dates and times, and evidence of reputational and business harm, both material and non-material. File the criminal complaint within the three-month period under Article 3 of the Criminal Procedure Law and seek compensatory civil damages.

267Someone published my phone number, home address or family information, followed by harassment. Which evidence should I organise?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Legal classification: Violation of private life, defamation, disclosure of confidential data and incitement to harassment, or doxxing, under Articles 438 and 434 of the Penal Code. Required: The post or story link, publishing account number, and images of intrusive or abusive messages and calls resulting from publication. Bring an urgent criminal case so the National Security Service can be contacted to block the account, summon the publisher and bring the matter to trial.

268Part of a recording or conversation was edited or published out of context. Which versions are needed to establish the full picture?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Classification and defence: Distorting context falls within defamation and fabrication of misleading evidence under Articles 433 and 255 of the Penal Code. For the lawyer: The complete uncut conversation or original raw recording and its chronology. Request forensic comparison of both versions to establish editing and bad faith, clear your position and pursue the publisher for misleading evidence and defamation.

269A page impersonates my professional identity or business and asks people for money. What helps protect my name and position?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Strict legal classification: Impersonation, misuse of a trade name and digital fraud under Article 456 of the Penal Code, and infringement of trade-name protection under Articles 20–26 of Commerce Law No. 30 of 1984, Trade Names and Commercial Register Regulation No. 6 of 1985 and the amended Companies Law No. 21 of 1997. For the lawyer: The trade-name certificate or professional ID, such as a bar card, the fake page link and screenshots of money demands to customers. Submit an urgent criminal complaint and an official legal notice protecting you from victims' financial claims, and contact the Communications and Media Commission for official closure of the page.

270Multiple accounts repeatedly attack me online. What helps distinguish the incidents and link accounts without unproven accusations?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Procedural classification: Organised online mobs constitute public defamation, insult and criminal participation under Articles 433 and 434 read with Article 48 of the Penal Code. Required: Collect the most active account links and copied comments, and complain against whoever the investigation identifies. The Investigation Court judge contacts the National Security Service and cybercrime authorities to trace IP addresses and identify the actual organiser or funder, without random allegations that could expose you to a false-accusation claim.

271I administer a group or page, and a member posted accusations or threats. What determines my position as administrator?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Administrator's legal position: Criminal responsibility is personal under Article 19 of the Constitution. The author is ordinarily directly responsible. Relevant questions: Did you delete the post or block the member immediately upon notification? This negates criminal intent and participation. Did you pin, engage with or praise it? This may expose you to participation by incitement or assistance under Article 48 of the Penal Code. Required: Preserve moderation activity logs establishing non-participation and prompt removal.

272The offending post was deleted or the account renamed, but I kept screenshots and old links. What determines their usefulness?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Procedural classification: Preserved digital evidence has recognised evidentiary force under Electronic Signature and Electronic Transactions Law No. 78 of 2012. Required: Screenshots showing the complete URL, permanent numerical account ID, date and time. The lawyer seeks a digital-evidence expert, technical comparison and recovery of cached material and server logs to establish the incident despite deletion.

273The abuse occurred through an overseas account or platform, and the account holder may be abroad. What determines jurisdiction and options?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Jurisdiction: Where the offence's result and harm occur within Iraq, Iraqi courts have territorial jurisdiction under Article 6 of the Penal Code. Routes: File with an Iraqi Investigation Court and give statements to seek an in-absentia arrest warrant and place the accused on airport and border arrival-watch lists. Seek an Interpol Red Notice where identity and overseas residence are established.

274Someone published private conversations, images or documents entrusted to them. What is needed to assess publication and consent?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Strict legal classification: Disclosure of secrets, breach of trust and violation of private life under Articles 437 and 438 of the Penal Code. Judicial principle: Providing images or conversations within a private or confidential relationship never gives the recipient a right to publish them publicly. For the lawyer: Establish the absence of express written consent, the publication link and relationship end date, to seek arrest, removal and material and non-material compensation.

275An employee or organisation leaked my data or files on social media. Which contracts and evidence should I prepare?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Legal classification: Disclosure of employment or professional secrets under Article 437 of the Penal Code, with contractual and tort liability requiring compensation. Required: The service contract or NDA, proof that the leaked data was exclusively held by that organisation, and the leak link. Pursue the employee criminally and the employer civilly for substantial financial compensation for resulting reputational or business harm.

276I allowed someone to use my account or device, but access or publication continued after the relationship ended. What determines consent's scope?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Legal classification: Unauthorised access after permission ends constitutes intrusion, data theft and privacy violation under Article 438 of the Penal Code. Scope of consent: Establish notification that the relationship and permission ended through an express message, and IP and device login logs proving access after withdrawal, for use in court.

277The other party submitted an audio recording made without my knowledge. What is needed to review its acquisition, integrity and context?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Iraqi cassation principle: Covert recordings in a private place are invalid under Article 438 of the Penal Code and Article 40 of the Constitution, and are not lawful evidence without prior Investigation Court permission. Cassation exception: The Court of Cassation has accepted a recording made by a participant to protect themselves from an offence directed against them, such as bribery or blackmail. Defence requirements: Immediately challenge admissibility and integrity, seek forensic examination for editing or AI-generated audio, and establish entrapment and distortion of context.

278Someone threatens publication or a complaint unless I pay a disputed debt. What distinguishes lawful recovery from threats?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Decisive distinction: Lawful recovery: Stating an intention to use proper legal routes, such as a civil claim or court complaint if a debt is unpaid. Threats and blackmail under Article 430: Threatening defamation, scandal, reputational harm or disclosure of images or correspondence to pressure payment of a disputed amount. Required: Messages involving defamation, extortion or exploitation of private matters can turn the incident from a civil debt dispute into financial blackmail, exposing the sender to criminal proceedings.

279A complaint relies on selected messages, but I deny threatening or blackmailing anyone. What does defence counsel need?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Procedural defence: Provide the entire conversation in chronological order to establish its true context. Show prior provocation or the financial dispute establishing that the words expressed anger or a rights claim rather than criminal intent to threaten or blackmail. Seek forensic examination of the phone to establish selective editing and misleading the court, and request rejection and closure under Article 130(B) of the Criminal Procedure Law.

280My minor child faces threats or blackmail through games or social media. What is needed while protecting the child's privacy?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Legal classification: A felony of exploitation of a minor and aggravated cyber-blackmail under the Penal Code and Juvenile Welfare Law No. 76 of 1983. Privacy and protection: Preserve the phone and conversations without embarrassing or reprimanding the child. The parent, father or mother, files the complaint as the minor's legal representative before the Investigation Court judge in a private hearing to protect the child and family's reputation. Coordinate with the National Security Service's anti-blackmail directorate to trace accounts, arrest the offender and immediately remove all material from their devices.

281Someone uses workplace authority to demand unwanted acts in return for work or avoiding harm. Which facts need assessment?
Answer as provided by the lawyer

Aggravated legal classification: Abuse of influence or public office and exploitation of a person in need under Articles 331 and 340 of the Penal Code, and non-monetary bribery and employment-related blackmail under Article 430. For the lawyer: Direct or implied messages, abusive administrative orders, threats of dismissal or negative evaluation, and colleagues' testimony. Approach the Federal Commission of Integrity's Investigations Department or competent Investigation Court judge confidentially for a judicially supervised operation to apprehend the official in the act and remove them from public office.
