Critical rules in major felony files and post-conviction review

Three procedural pillars governing cassation review, sentence reduction and amnesty:

1. Second General Amnesty Amendment Law No. 2 of 2025

Published in Official Gazette issue 4814 on 18 February 2025, enabling re-investigation or retrial applications where convictions rested on coerced confessions, secret informants or uncorroborated co-defendants, and covering personal-use narcotics possession up to 50 grams under strict statutory conditions.

2. Reclassifying the offence and testing forensic evidence

A decisive legal line separates capital murder under Penal Code Article 406 from Article 405 intentional homicide or Article 410 fatal assault, and separates drug trafficking (Articles 27–28) from personal possession (Article 32), based on autopsy findings and seizure records.

3. Bona-fide third-party assets and final sentence reduction

Penal Code Article 101 protects bona-fide owners against confiscation of vehicles and property, while Penal Code Articles 142–143 and Criminal Procedure Law Articles 270–279 and 331 govern sentence merger, the 25-year aggregate imprisonment cap, retrial and conditional release.

Specialist Litigation Scope & Governing Iraqi Legislation

Procedural classification grounded in Iraqi legislation in force and Federal Court of Cassation jurisprudence:

Sector 01

Second General Amnesty Amendment Law No. 2 of 2025 & Judicial Committees

File review for sentenced prisoners and wanted persons under Law No. 2 of 2025 (Official Gazette issue 4814) and Executive Instructions No. 1 of 2025, filing coverage and re-investigation or retrial applications before the competent judicial committees, challenging committee refusals before the Federal Court of Cassation, and completing public-funds financial settlements.

Sector 02

Intentional Homicide, Death Penalty & Life Sentence Cassation (Arts. 405, 406, 410)

Challenging premeditation and aggravating elements under Penal Code Article 406 to seek reclassification to Article 405 or Article 410, establishing legitimate self-defence (Articles 42–46), cross-examining autopsy and crime-scene inconsistencies, and authenticating heirs' waivers and Minors' Care Directorate approvals under Law No. 78 of 1980 for judicial mitigation (Articles 130–132).

Sector 03

Major Narcotics Felonies, Medical Warehouses & Confiscated Asset Recovery (Law 50/2017)

Technical defence distinguishing trafficking (Articles 27–28) from personal possession (Article 32), challenging unlawful searches and laboratory weight discrepancies, representing pharmacies, medical warehouses and chemical precursor importers under Instructions No. 2 of 2026, and recovering confiscated vehicles, real estate and funds belonging to bona-fide third parties under Penal Code Article 101.

Sector 04

Anti-Terrorism Law Cases, Kidnapping, Armed Disputes & In-Absentia Sentences

Contesting specific terrorist intent under Anti-Terrorism Law No. 13 of 2005 to return ordinary offences to Penal Code jurisdiction, excluding uncorroborated co-defendant statements (Article 125 CPC) and coerced confessions (Article 127 CPC; Article 37(1)(c) Constitution), managing voluntary surrender and mandatory retrial of in-absentia felony sentences (Article 247 CPC), and clearing airport/border mistaken-identity holds.

Sector 05

Post-Conviction Relief: Sentence Merger, 25-Year Cap, Retrial & Conditional Release

Auditing final convictions to apply sentence merger for inseparable offences carrying execution of the severest penalty alone (Penal Code Article 142), enforcing the 25-year statutory cap on consecutive prison terms and absorption of detention by imprisonment (Article 143(A) and (C)), filing correction of cassation decisions (Article 266 CPC), retrial petitions based on new decisive evidence or false testimony (Articles 270–279 CPC), and conditional release (Article 331 CPC).

Procedural Questions, Statutory Defences & Practical Answers

Procedural answers supported by Iraqi legislation in force and Official Gazette citations:

When are narcotics cases covered by the Second General Amnesty Amendment Law No. 2 of 2025?

Under Law No. 2 of 2025 (published in Official Gazette issue 4814 on 18 February 2025), drug trafficking offences (Articles 27 and 28) remain excluded, whereas possession of narcotics or psychotropic substances for personal use is eligible where the net seized quantity does not exceed 50 grams, provided the offence predates the law's entry into force and the person was not previously granted amnesty or previously convicted of a narcotics offence.

Source: Second General Amnesty Amendment Law No. 2 of 2025 (Official Gazette issue 4814)
How does a convicted person request re-investigation or retrial for a coerced confession or secret informant under the 2025 Amnesty Law?

Law No. 2 of 2025 allows persons convicted of felonies or misdemeanours to apply before judicial committees formed in the Courts of Appeal for re-investigation or retrial where the conviction rested on a coerced confession, a confidential informant or an uncorroborated co-defendant statement. The committee examines the original case file, medical reports and evidentiary contradictions, and its decision may be challenged before the Federal Court of Cassation within 30 days.

Source: Law No. 2 of 2025 and Articles 125 & 127 of the Criminal Procedure Law
When can a capital murder charge under Article 406 be downgraded to Article 405 or Article 410?

The trial court or Criminal Panel of the Court of Cassation reclassifies a charge from Penal Code Article 406(1) to Article 405 where premeditation or lying in wait is disproved, such as in a sudden, unpremeditated altercation. The charge is downgraded to fatal assault under Article 410 where forensic autopsy findings, the instrument used and the injury site demonstrate intent to injure rather than intent to kill.

Source: Articles 33, 405, 406 and 410 of amended Penal Code No. 111 of 1969
What legal effect does a tribal settlement and heirs' waiver have in a homicide case when minor heirs exist?

An informal tribal settlement document alone has no judicial effect unless all adult legal heirs record an express judicial waiver before the court. Where the deceased left minor children under 18, Iraqi law requires a guardianship certificate and formal approval from the Minors' Care Directorate under Law No. 78 of 1980 after depositing the minors' compensation shares. Once completed, the court may apply statutory mitigation under Penal Code Articles 130–132.

Source: Penal Code Articles 130–132 and Minors' Care Law No. 78 of 1980
The court confiscated my vehicle or property in another person's narcotics case without my knowledge. How do I recover it?

Article 101 of the Iraqi Penal Code expressly provides that confiscation of items obtained from or used in a crime operates without prejudice to the rights of bona-fide third parties. Where the registered owner establishes title (vehicle registration or Tapu deed) and lack of knowledge of or participation in the offence, counsel files a restitution application and an independent cassation petition to quash the confiscation clause and release the asset.

Source: Article 101 of amended Iraqi Penal Code No. 111 of 1969
When is a kidnapping or armed dispute removed from the Anti-Terrorism Law and tried under the ordinary Penal Code?

Application of Anti-Terrorism Law No. 13 of 2005 requires specific terrorist intent aimed at spreading public terror or destabilising public security. Where investigation evidence establishes a purely personal, commercial or family dispute directed at a specific individual without terrorist motive, defence counsel challenges jurisdiction and classification so the case is tried under the ordinary Penal Code (such as Articles 421, 430 or 413).

Source: Anti-Terrorism Law No. 13 of 2005 and Penal Code No. 111 of 1969
Can a felony conviction rest solely on another co-defendant's statement without corroborating evidence?

Under Articles 125 and 213(C) of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971 and settled Federal Court of Cassation jurisprudence, an uncorroborated statement by one co-defendant against another is insufficient on its own to sustain a felony conviction unless supported by independent material or circumstantial evidence, particularly where the accused consistently denied the charge.

Source: Articles 125 and 213 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971
A prisoner has multiple sentences totalling 40 years. How do sentence merger and the 25-year statutory cap work?

Iraqi law provides two statutory mechanisms: first, sentence merger under Penal Code Article 142 where offences committed for a single purpose are indivisibly connected, resulting in execution of the severest sentence alone; second, for distinct offences under Article 143(A) and (C), imprisonment absorbs detention terms by its length, and total consecutive imprisonment executed may not exceed a statutory maximum of 25 years.

Source: Articles 142 and 143 of amended Penal Code No. 111 of 1969
An in-absentia death or life sentence was issued while the accused was abroad. What happens upon voluntary surrender?

Under Article 247 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971, an in-absentia felony sentence is suspended upon the person's appearance or arrest, and the Criminal Court must conduct a full de novo trial in the defendant's presence, hearing defence witnesses and alibi evidence, with full authority to acquit, discharge or reclassify the charge.

Source: Article 247 of Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971
How can a prisoner's family in Iraq or abroad appoint a lawyer to review a felony case file?

A formal power of attorney for an inmate is executed inside the correctional facility before the delegated notary public under Notaries Law No. 33 of 1998. Relatives residing in the United States, Europe or the Gulf may coordinate directly with our Baghdad practice and settle agreed fees through the practice's official US bank account at JPMorgan Chase.

Source: Advocates Law No. 173 of 1965 and Notaries Law No. 33 of 1998
