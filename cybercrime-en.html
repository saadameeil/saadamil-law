Immediate safety guide for victims of digital blackmail

Three essential steps to protect your legal position and help formally document the incident:

1. Do not transfer money; preserve the record

Do not transfer any money, whatever the threat. Do not delete the conversations or account: conversations are among the principal digital indications used to establish the incident before the investigating judge.

2. Professional confidentiality and privacy protection

The lawyer submits a formal request to the competent investigating judge to record statements in a closed session, taking the available judicial measures to protect privacy and personal standing.

3. Obtaining digital fingerprints and IP data

Addressing the Communications and Media Commission and telephone companies to obtain judicial orders for technical and security authorities to trace connection data and formally pursue the offender.

Cybercrime and blackmail legal questions

Procedural answers based on the Iraqi Penal Code and decisions of the Court of Cassation's Criminal Panel:

Someone is blackmailing me with private photos and conversations and demanding money. What is the first essential step to protect myself?

The essential rule: do not delete the conversations or account, and do not send money. Deleting conversations destroys your digital evidence; paying encourages the blackmailer to keep demanding more.

Immediately back up the conversations and links, then consult the lawyer to bring an urgent complaint before the competent Investigation Court and refer the file to the digital forensic evidence unit to preserve technical records and obtain a formal arrest warrant.

Source: Article 430 of amended Iraqi Penal Code No. 111 of 1969
Will my family or acquaintances learn about the complaint? What professional confidentiality duties does the lawyer have?

Lawyer Saad Amil's practice observes professional confidentiality protected by Article 42 of Iraqi Advocacy Law No. 173 of 1965.

The application is formally submitted to the competent investigating judge, and statements are recorded under the procedures governing criminal complaints. Legal professional confidentiality is fully observed, and available measures are taken to protect privacy within formal judicial procedures.

Source: Advocacy Law Article 42 and Supreme Judicial Council instructions on confidentiality of blackmail complaints
What is the penalty for online blackmail under the Iraqi Penal Code?

Digital blackmail is often classified as a felony under Article 430 of amended Iraqi Penal Code No. 111 of 1969, carrying imprisonment for up to seven years if the threat accompanies a demand for money or a dishonourable act.

If the threat is unaccompanied by a financial demand, it is punishable by imprisonment under Penal Code Article 431. The penalty is aggravated if the offender exploits a position of trust or an official role in relation to the victim.

Source: Articles 430 and 431 of Iraqi Penal Code No. 111 of 1969
The blackmailer uses a fake account and I do not know their name. Can they be identified and arrested?

The formal route is to obtain an order from the competent Investigation Court addressing the Communications and Media Commission, mobile telephone companies and internet service providers.

Requests cover digital fingerprints, IP addresses, login records and available technical data, together with formal judicial orders to secure digital evidence and pursue the offender under the law.

Source: Criminal Panel decisions of the Federal Court of Cassation on digital evidence
Are screenshots sufficient evidence before the Investigation Court?

Screenshots are preliminary indications the court may accept to start an investigation. Their evidential value is strengthened when a digital forensic expert examines the phone or device, extracts messages, matches digital fingerprints and formally documents the findings.

This documentation supports the evidential position before the investigating authority and helps address possible arguments about alteration or fabrication. Final assessment of the evidence's integrity and weight remains within the discretion of the court and competent investigating judge.

Source: Article 213 of amended Criminal Procedure Law No. 23 of 1971
I face insults and a defamation campaign on Facebook and TikTok. What legal route can help?

We pursue two judicial routes:

1. Criminal: A complaint before the Investigation Court under Penal Code Articles 433 and 434 seeking conviction, imprisonment, a fine, removal of posts and blocking of abusive accounts.
2. Civil: A compensation claim before the Court of First Instance under Iraqi Civil Code Article 205 for non-material harm and damage to social standing and professional reputation.

Source: Penal Code Articles 433 and 434 + Iraqi Civil Code Article 205
