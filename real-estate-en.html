Property buyer and heir checklist

Three essential procedural rules to strengthen your property position:

1. Overcoming co-owners' refusal

No heir or co-owner may block the sale of jointly owned property. Article 1070 of the Civil Code allows even the smallest co-owner to seek public auction and distribution of the proceeds in cash.

2. Conditions for applying Decision 1198

To obtain registration of a house bought through a private agreement despite the seller's refusal, confirm receipt of the price and your actual residence in the property, and avoid contractual penalties permitting withdrawal.

3. Protecting expatriates' property

Periodically check the property register and record a precautionary attachment where a dispute exists, to prevent exploitation of forged powers of attorney and unlawful occupation.

Property and co-ownership legal questions

Procedural answers based on the Civil Code and Federal Court of Cassation decisions:

Can a co-owner with a small share have a property sold and end co-ownership if the other heirs refuse?

Yes, by operation of law. Article 1070 of Iraqi Civil Code No. 40 of 1951 provides that any co-owner may seek to end co-ownership where the property cannot be divided in kind, such as a house, flat or shop.

Majority co-owners have no right to refuse or delay. The Court of First Instance issues a final judgment referring the property for sale by public auction through the Enforcement Directorate, with the proceeds distributed in cash according to the legal shares in the inheritance certificate.

Source: Article 1070 of Iraqi Civil Code No. 40 of 1951, as amended
How can ownership of a house purchased through a private written agreement be registered under Revolutionary Command Council Decision No. 1198 of 1977?

An ownership registration claim is brought before the Court of First Instance with territorial jurisdiction under Decision No. 1198 of 1977, as amended by Decision No. 1426 of 1983. Registration must be ordered where two essential conditions recognised by cassation case law are met:

1. The seller received the entire price or a substantial part of it.
2. The buyer lived in the house or added structures or plantings that cannot be removed without serious damage.

Once established, the court orders registration in the buyer's name at the Property Registration Directorate despite the seller's refusal and without their signature.

Source: Revolutionary Command Council Decision No. 1198 of 1977 and Federal Court of Cassation applications
What prevents application of Decision No. 1198 of 1977 and leads to dismissal of an ownership registration claim?

The ownership registration claim is dismissed and the buyer loses the right to acquire the property itself in the following cases:

1. The property cannot legally be transferred, such as land allocated for public benefit or a green belt.
2. The buyer did not actually live continuously in the property and merely took temporary possession or received it vacant.
3. The private agreement contains an express penalty clause allowing either party to withdraw in return for paying a penalty.
4. The seller was a co-owner and the other property co-owners did not sign.

Source: General Assembly decisions of the Federal Court of Cassation concerning Decision 1198
One heir occupies the property and refuses to leave. How can I claim rent for previous years?

We bring a claim for equivalent rental value under Article 1165 of the Iraqi Civil Code before the competent Court of First Instance.

The court appoints a judicial expert to assess the area's rental value for the entire previous period during which you were deprived of use of your ownership share. The occupier is ordered to pay your full entitlement, together with costs and legal fees.

Source: Article 1165 of the Iraqi Civil Code + Abd al-Rahman al-Allam's commentary
What is the statutory notarial notice period for evicting a tenant who fails to pay rent?

Under Property Rent Law No. 87 of 1979, amended by Law No. 56 of 2000 and the 2007 amendment (Iraqi Official Gazette issue 4035), eviction requires a notarial notice after rent becomes due.

The tenant is given a statutory period of 15 days starting the day after formal service to pay the arrears. If the 15 days expire without payment, an eviction claim is brought before the Court of First Instance, and the judge orders the tenant to vacate and deliver the property free of occupants.

Source: Property Rent Law No. 87 of 1979 and its amendments in force

Can an estate property be sold if one heir is a minor under 18?

Property containing a minor's share may not be sold without prior formal permission from the Personal Status Court and approval from the Minors' Care Directorate under Minors' Care Law No. 78 of 1980 (Iraqi Official Gazette issue 2772).

The sale must provide a clear benefit to the minor, and their entire share of the proceeds must be deposited in their account with the fund. No other heir may receive it, to prevent disposal of orphans' funds.

Source: Minors' Care Law No. 78 of 1980
